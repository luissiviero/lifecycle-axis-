## The three choices that are still the owner's

The adversarial round and the author reached the same position on every dissent. These three remain the owner's
because each reverses or extends an answer the owner gave before.

**1. The CI failure triage: keep it label-gated, or remove it.**
Options: (a) keep it behind the `triage` label, as approved on 2026-09-09; (b) remove it.
Recommendation: (a), because nothing else in the kit can read a failing job log (the `@claude` route forbids
Bash), and changing an approved answer is a revision of `ci-budget`, not a row in a review. The cost of (a) is zero
while nobody applies the label.

**2. The standing grant: wanted, or is one tap per item the floor?**
Options: (a) a standing grant for `risk-class: low` in the policy file, after the `risk-class` guard (row 14.1)
exists; (b) keep one grant tap per item.
Recommendation: build the guard either way, now, because the hook today guards three of the four grant keys and
a decision record says four. Then choose (a) only after the queue has advanced on its own at least once (row
100.1), because a standing grant on a queue that does not advance buys nothing and widens what runs unattended.

**3. The advance push under branch protection: a bypass actor, or a bot-opened pull request.**
Options: (a) a ruleset bypass for `github-actions[bot]` so the advance and index commits push straight to `main`;
(b) the bot opens and merges a pull request for them.
Recommendation: (a), because it is one setting and keeps the merge script as it is; (b) is more code and one more
workflow run per merge. Document the bypass in `github-setup.md` and have the merge script's dry run check it.

## What not to re-derive

The four evidence reports and the two review reports name every file they read and every command they ran. A new
session should cite them, not repeat them. The one thing this handoff cannot carry is the conversation's tone; the
review's stances A–G are the closest written form of it.
